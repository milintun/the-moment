
The process of developing The Moment was incredibly rewarding. Since it was an entirely open-ended project, it was challenging to make design decisions with tradeoffs. I decided not to implement a moderating concept because art shouldn’t be censored, and I accepted that if the site was overrun by bots and spam, that was an artistic statement itself. I sacrificed potential usability and safety for conceptual integrity. When I decided to cut profile pages, comments, and “my saved” works, I chose potential user confusion and frustration over familiarity for the sake of developing a collectivist rather than an individualistic platform. Because I made these design decisions before testing on any users, I simply chose what was most reasonable to me. It will require further testing and tuning to truly finalize where on these spectrums the app must lie in order to be simultaneously engaging, pleasant, novel, and safe.

Another difficult skill that I improved significantly on was concept design. This was the entirety of P1, but I realized that I was practicing it throughout implementation and iteration. I strongly believe that, unlike many aspects of implementation, concept design is not an automatable process. It is a direct expression of what I want my app to be, which cannot be executed well with an LLM. I spent most of my time refining my concepts and thinking of how they compose together to create functionality in The Moment. It was exciting to see that my newer P3 concepts were built on my P1 concepts despite me having a very limited sense of what my P1 concepts would be used for when I wrote them. For instance, my Nominating concept used my Upvoting and Cycling concept for posts. These kinds of planning and designing required a critical human eye, however LLMs were incredibly helpful for creating comprehensive test suites and using my concepts to write code. 

One mistake that I made was allowing an LLM to implement a vague UI/UX design description for my P3. I had a broad idea of my visual design system—aesthetic, color, navigation—but it was not specific enough. The LLM was forced to make many design decisions about layout, visual hierarchy, etc. This was good enough for my P3 iteration, but I was met with that familiar feeling that a design looks off without knowing exactly why. I essentially started from scratch in P4 and P5 and redid designs on almost every page. Instead of being a helpful starting point, the generated design made the process incredibly challenging because it was hard to imagine creative alternative designs when there was a mediocre one that worked right in front of me. This puts visual design with the set of tasks that are not suitable for an LLM to complete.
